Clock step 2100 MHz — mining mode, 2026-09-29 (HANDOFF item 1)
Question: can the Balanced cap move from 2000 to 2100 MHz (power <= 75 W, board < 90 C, more T-MAC/s)?
Answer: NO. 2100 MHz is thermally fine but costs ~+5 W, crosses the 75 W target and gives no measurable gain.

Setup: real miner (spark-pearl-miner 0.1.0-alpha.2, main a442750) under my local 'mineracao' mode, GPU 100 %
dedicated, Balanced (75 W target, 85 W stop), Kryptex (prl-br.kryptex.network:8048, TLS, v2/gzip). The clock lock
was changed live with a small local root helper (nvidia-smi -lgc 300,2100 / 300,2000), without restarting the miner.
Clock under load: 1976 MHz at the 2000 cap, 2086 MHz at the 2100 cap (+5.6 %). The desktop session stayed up.

Reading: at the 2000 MHz cap the attempt loop is already not kernel-bound. The miner keeps the SMs busy only 87-93 %
of the time; at 2100 MHz the kernel finishes sooner, the SM-busy share drops by ~7 points and the credited rate does
not move. The time saved in the kernel is spent waiting on something else (host side of the attempt loop or the
shared SoC). Desktop GPU clients (the Claude desktop app, Chrome, Xorg) took 0.4-4 % SM on average per phase (up to
~10 % in single samples while a chat was streaming); that explains only part of the plateaus.

Decision: keep the Balanced cap at 2000 MHz (no change to code, docs or the local unit). Next lever instead of clock:
measure the per-attempt gaps and push the SM-busy share towards ~98 % (worth +5-10 % at the same clock and power).
Side effect: the daemon's clock-cap detector now reports 'uncapped' (it saw 2086 MHz) until the next restart; the lock
is back at 300-2000 MHz (1976 MHz under load at 22:45).
